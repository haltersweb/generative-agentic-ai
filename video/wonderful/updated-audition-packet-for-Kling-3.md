## Settings and verified platform capabilities

Choose VIDEO 3.0, image-to-video, Native Audio ON and Multi-Shot OFF. Use 16:9 landscape, with the same available resolution for every comparison. Choose 15 seconds for Potter clips; use the durations below for the living-room clips. Image-to-video determines the scene framing from the starting image, so a studio headshot alone is not the finished scene input.

Kling documents 3–15-second generation, native audio, single-shot output when Multi-Shot is disabled, and start-frame plus Element referencing. Its Element workflow can use 2–4 reference images and a voice assignment. If a voice is already bound, do not redefine it in the video prompt. These features support continuity but do not guarantee it. [Official VIDEO 3.0 guide](https://kling.ai/quickstart/klingai-video-3-model-user-guide)

The shot lengths, test order and performance instructions here are our production choices, not platform guarantees. Avoid treating approximate performance beats as frame-accurate control.

## Dialogue, action and editing

The excerpts preserve the director's draft dialogue on PDF pages 26–27 and 35. Potter's opening exposition and the denunciation after the first refusals are intentionally outside this test. The living-room selection starts after the teacher call and ends at George's exit. No new screenplay dialogue is introduced.

P1 has the densest speech. If it comes out rushed, divide it at George's unfinished “Three hundred and —” and give Potter's remaining response its own listening clip. Apply the same split to both candidates. Add time rather than rewrite or accelerate the lines.

The handshake stays entirely within P4 so a cut cannot manufacture the refusal. The stopped throw stays within M2, destruction within M3, and the discovery of the family's fear and apology within M4. These are deliberately separate acting units. Their assembled sequence can compare interpretations, but cannot establish that a performer sustained a single uninterrupted minute.

Review individual clips before assembling. Cut at the planned boundaries, remove only redundant opening/closing stillness, and do not rearrange reaction order. Keep room tone and volume comparable; no score or cutaways to invented family reactions. An edited reaction that looks moving can conceal a weak generated performance.

## Continuity checks before accepting a take

- Candidate face and apparent age remain stable; no change to jaw, nose, eyes, hairline or build.
- George's left ear remains deaf, no visible hearing aid, consistent right-side listening orientation. Do not mirror the image between clips.
- Same provisional George voice; Potter's voice stays distinct and off-camera. Dialogue is exact, in order, with correct speaker attribution.
- Potter: same jacket, shirt, cup positions and light; normal right-hand anatomy and contact; refusal follows contact, never precedes it.
- Home: knob begins in right hand, is placed on piano in M2, stays there. Four models begin intact, break only in M3, remain broken. No regeneration of models or extra destruction.
- Piano stops at M1's command and never resumes. Coat is on the chair until M4, then carried out. Petals remain in its pocket, unseen. No bloodied lip yet.
- Shame follows the family's silence. Reject early pleading expressions that soften the outburst before it happens.
