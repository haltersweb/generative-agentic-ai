## 1. Intent and the moment

**Intent.** George came back for his hat, and that's all he'll admit to. Mary wants him and has stopped pretending she doesn't. Sam's call offers George the world he's always wanted, and to hear it he has to stand with his cheek against hers. The longer the world talks, the less he hears it.

**Whose scene:** George's. Mary drives the first third (the smash, the performance, the choice). From the moment she pulls him in, the reactions and the last look are his.

**The moment:** Mary turns her face to his and whispers, "He says it's the chance of a lifetime," and George breaks. (S41 → S42.)

**The turn:** "We can both hear. Come here." (S19). Mary chooses not to go to the extension. Everything after is the trap closing.

## 2. Weight audit (where the original's force comes from)

| Source | In the 1946 scene (from the shot log) | How the vertical version carries it |
|---|---|---|
| **Duration** *(main source)* | One locked two-shot held 1:54 (55% of the scene) through Sam's pitch, the drop, the grab and the outburst. No escape for them or for us. | We can't hold 1:54 on a phone, or in Kling. **No-escape cutting replaces it:** from S20 to S42, 23 shots stay inside the shared-receiver space, and every cut is the same size or closer (MS → MCU → CU → ECU). Nothing cuts out until the phone drops. Sam's voice runs unbroken across those cuts, so **the ear hears one take while the eye keeps moving closer.** |
| **Composition** | Two faces side by side, inches apart, receiver between. | **The stack.** George is taller, so he has to bend down into her frame: his face upper, hers tucked under his jaw, the earpiece between their cheeks. The empty dark above his head is the world he wants. He gives it up by bending down, and when he straightens for the outburst it's above him again. |
| **Performance** | We read George's discomfort and Mary's watching inside one frame. | CU singles carved from the stack. **Sam talks over silent faces:** all 17 of Sam's shots play on a listener, so the reaction carries each beat. |
| **Withholding** | Sam is seen once, for 5.8 s. | Sam is never seen. The world George wants is only a voice in a black receiver. |
| **Space** | A busy hall; the stairs and Mrs. Hatch give height; George deep behind Mary at the door (O5). | Two setups set the axis (phone ↔ door), then the space collapses to the stack. George's posture replaces the stairs as the height device. No deep two-shot at the door: we keep them in separate frames until she pulls him in. |
| **Objects** | The record (Mary's anger); the phone (the trap). | Both, plus **the hat**, our addition. It's planted on the hook (S04), taken (S06), crushed in his fist as Sam pitches (S28), dropped with the receiver (S43), and paid off on the floor (S53). George came back for it; it's what he leaves on the floor. (SD-015: plant before payoff.) |
| **Sound and silence** | From the sides: the record, the ringing, Sam's voice, the crash. (I haven't analyzed the original's audio mix.) | The smash kills the music, leaving only the ring. Sam is the only "world" sound. **The one deliberate silence is S42**, George after the whisper, the scene's longest hold. The crash is a smash cut. |
| **Cutting pattern** | 13 busy shots in 73 s, then the hold; **the release is the first cut after it.** | A brisk opening (~3.0 s average), then the vise (~4.1 s average), with longer shots that keep getting closer. The break is a smash cut, followed by shorter, angrier cross-cutting (~3.6 s). **The release is the first widening since S13** (S49 MS embrace), the vertical equivalent of the original's first cut. |

**What can't carry over:** the real-time endurance of one unbroken take. The replacement is continuous sound plus steadily tightening sizes. If the test renders show the cuts breaking the spell, we cut fewer, longer shots in the vise rather than more.

## 6. Production risks and what I'd test first

1. **Two faces in one Kling clip.** The stack (S20–S23, S35, S41) needs both actors in one frame with stable identities. Only one of them speaks, or neither does while Sam's voice plays. Our verified cases so far are one actor plus a hand. **Before the full board, I'd ask Jean for two test clips:** S22 (both silent, Sam's voice) and S41 (Mary whispers, ECU, lips almost touching). If the stack won't hold, the fallback is singles carved tighter, each with only a sliver of the other (hair, temple, jaw) at the frame edge. The vise still works that way; it just loses some heat.
2. **Physical contact** (S20 pull, S44 grab, S49 embrace). Each has a fallback on the card: start mid-action from a master still, and let a cut on action hide the contact.
3. **The record smash** (S02). Breakage plus a hand on a prop. The fallback is that the smash happens on the cut, carried by sound.
4. **Episode length.** At 3:08 the scene is slightly over the usual 1–3 min episode. See decision 3.

## 7. Decisions for Adina

1. **Look: black-and-white or color?** *I recommend black-and-white.* It's a 1946 recreation, it keeps the light on the faces, and it saves color for the modern version to own.
2. **Who plays George and Mary?** We have no Mary reference, and I need to know whether George uses Daniel Reyes's Element or someone else. Either way, the performers are directed as themselves, never toward the 1946 actors.
3. **One episode or two?** As one episode it runs 3:08. As two, Episode 1 runs 0:57 and ends on "We can both hear. Come here." with George's face as she reaches for him, which is a strong cliffhanger. Episode 2 opens by re-landing that line and runs ~2:11. *I recommend two if this goes to a platform, one if it's a showcase piece.*

**Also open:** the sides end on the embrace and the names, so I've ended on George's last look, with no kiss. If you want the kiss, it would go between S52 and S53.
