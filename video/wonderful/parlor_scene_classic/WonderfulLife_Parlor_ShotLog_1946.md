## What the log tells us

1. **The scene is one held breath.** After 73 s of fairly busy coverage (13 shots, three of them Mrs. Hatch), the film locks into a single 1:54 two-shot and never cuts until George breaks. The audience is trapped at that distance with them. The tension is that *nobody cuts away*: not the camera, not George.
2. **The explosion happens inside the held take.** The phone drop, the grab and the outburst all play in O11 with no cut. The first cut after 1:54 comes *after* the anger, on Mary's tears. The release is a cut; the tension is the absence of one.
3. **Mrs. Hatch is the only other cutting rhythm.** She gets 3 of 14 shots, the only high angles, and the comic button. Removing her removes most of the scene's early cutting and its vertical "height" (the stairs). We'll need our own height device.
4. **Deep staging is already in the original (O5).** Mary performs for a George standing deep in the background. That's a natural vertical stack. We'll reinterpret the idea rather than copy the frame.
5. **Sam is barely seen** (5.8 s), so playing him entirely as an offscreen voice costs little.

## Implication for vertical

We can't hold one 1:54 shot. A phone viewer won't sit through it, and Kling clips run to about 12 s. Our version has to make **cuts feel like no escape**: every cut goes *closer*, never away. That means coverage only inside the shared-receiver space (two faces, mouths, the receiver, hands, breath), with frames tightening as Sam talks, and the first cut *out* of that space saved for the release. This is the core of the weight transfer, to be developed in the weight audit.
