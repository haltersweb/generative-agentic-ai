## Notes on each change

| # | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| J1 | **Keep** the record smash. | It's Mary's anger in a single gesture, and our only anger before George's. "Buffalo Gals" is a traditional song, but we'd generate or record our own rendition, not lift the film's audio. |
| J2 | **Cut** Mrs. Hatch's "Mary, he's waiting!" | Your decision. "The phone is still ringing" now carries the push. |
| J3 | **Keep** "I forgot my hat." | The hat is our stakes object: George is leaving and not leaving. In the original, Mary performs her call *for* a George standing deep behind her (log O5). |
| J4 | **Sam becomes SAM'S VOICE throughout; cut the NY office cutaway** (header, action line, "(into phone)" and the return slug). | His only on-screen moment was 5.8 s (log O8). Staying in the hall keeps us trapped with them. It also removes a location, Sam and the extras. *Header changes only; no spoken words change.* |
| J5 | **Cut** Mrs. Hatch's interruption, Mary's reply and the stammered "Geo…". Mary calls "George!" once, then "Sam wants to speak to you." | The stammer existed because Mrs. Hatch had cut her off. Without the interruption it has no cause. The surviving words are unchanged. |
| J6 | **Keep** Sam's "get on the extension"; **cut** "Mother's on the extension." | This is the key change. Sam asks for the extension, and Mary simply doesn't go. "We can both hear. Come here." becomes her choice, and the audience sees it is one. |
| J7 | **Keep as written** (33 words). | I looked for a clean cut-only trim and there isn't one. Sam talks fast, so it plays in ~11 s across two shots. An optional reword is offered below. |
| J8 | **Keep in full.** Revises my earlier "maybe shortened." | Offered the world, George's first instinct is to bring it home to Bedford Falls: "the labor… half the town." He has already chosen without knowing it. It splits naturally into two shots. |
| J9 | **Cut** "Oh, baby, I knew you'd come through. Now, here's the point. Mary, Mary, you're in on this too." (19 words) | It's filler between the hook and the money question. "Now listen. Have you got any money?" lands harder. |
| J10 | **Cut** "Well, now listen." Keep the rest. | J9 keeps "Now listen," so this would repeat it. Every other phrase here is echoed in George's outburst ("ground floor," "married"), so it all stays. |
| J11 | **Keep the outburst and the release word for word.** | It's the payoff. George throws Sam's words back ("plastics," "ground floors," "married") and breaks on "and you're…". |

Word counts are computed from the markup. Time is an estimate at ~2.9 words/s, plus room for the record smash, the hand-off and the embrace. The board will set the real runtime.

**Why the trim is light.** The original's tension is a 1:54 unbroken take of Sam's pitch playing over two faces that can't move apart (log O11). Sam's talk is the fuse. Cutting it hard would shorten the time the closeness has to build, and George's explosion would come too soon. I trimmed only the filler.

## Optional rewords (not in the markup; your call)

These would add words not in the 1946 script, so I've kept them out by default.

- **J7, Sam.** Original: "George, you remember that night in Martini's bar when you told me you read someplace about making plastics out of soybeans?" Possible: "George, you remember when you told me about making plastics out of soybeans?" This saves ~8 words, but loses Martini's, a nice piece of Bedford Falls texture.
- **J5, Mary.** If a single "George!" feels abrupt in performance, we could restore the stammer as an internal hesitation: "Geo... George, Sam wants to speak to you." That's the original wording, now read as Mary's nerves rather than an interruption. My lean is to try it this way.
