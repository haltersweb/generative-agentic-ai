## Test 1 — Potter's offer — target 60 seconds

Casting objective: Does he mentally accept before the handshake? Can the first refusal precede an articulate moral justification?

Scene state: George 34, 2019, daytime glass conference room. Soft cool window light, restrained fill, no sinister lighting on Potter. George wears a modest charcoal jacket and pale blue open-collar shirt; clean-shaven, fresh but ordinary haircut, wedding band. Seated eye-level medium close-up, 50mm lens impression, frame wide enough to retain the handshake at its lower edge. Potter is a neutral off-camera reader to George's right, with only a hand entering frame. Static camera; George may rise within a planned frame near the end. Two coffees and a legal pad showing $310,000. No cigar: this draft has coffee.

Excerpt: begin on the salary figure being turned toward George; finish at the second sequence of refusals, before the long denunciation. This is a shortened audition excerpt with the opening exposition and concluding speech omitted, not rewritten dialogue.

POTTER: To manage my properties. Three-year contract. Starting Monday.

GEORGE: Three hundred and —

POTTER: You'd hate it, of course. The best house in town. Your wife in clothes she didn't order off a screen. Real trips, two a year.

GEORGE: Would I. You're talking to me. George Bailey. You're sure?

POTTER: The same. Whose ship has finally come in, if he has the sense to board it.

GEORGE: What happens to Bailey?

POTTER: Are you afraid of winning, George? Three years. Yes or no.

GEORGE: I know I ought to be saying yes. Could I have a day? Just to talk to Mary.

POTTER: Of course. Go home. I'll have the papers drawn.

[Potter stands and offers his hand. George takes it.]

GEORGE: No.

POTTER: Take the day. There's no —

GEORGE: No. No — wait. Wait a minute.

Timing is approximate and includes overlapping conversational pickups. Do not accelerate the handshake to hit an exact runtime; extend slightly if necessary to preserve the beat.

### Reading 1A — Daniel Reyes

Inner objective: Let this man finally acknowledge what I am worth.

- 0–12 seconds: Read the number twice. A held breath releases through the nose. Look up to check Potter is serious. The shoulders settle before the smile appears.
- 12–28 seconds: Enjoy the offer openly. On “You're talking to me,” sustain eye contact with Potter. The almost-laugh is an involuntary release, not mockery. For a few seconds he speaks as Potter's equal.
- 28–42 seconds: Ask about Bailey as a practical condition to settle, not a trap. At “Are you afraid of winning,” start to answer, swallow it, and negotiate for the day. He already imagines bringing good news home.
- 42–52 seconds: Rise readily and take the hand with ordinary business confidence. Let the smile disappear while contact remains. The first “No” is low and surprising to him.
- 52–60 seconds: Look from the hand to Potter. The repeated refusals gather speed; conviction has not caught up. Finish on the struggle to stop an agreement already underway.

Watch for: the pleasure of being valued must be visible without a smug transformation. Fail the interpretation if he looks suspicious of Potter throughout or arrives at the handshake morally prepared.

Identity risks: generic leading-man refinement, changing nose bend or jaw width, overly glamorous grooming, accidental age 40 instead of 34. Preserve the deeper cheek crease and compact broad upper body.

### Reading 1B — Adrian Shaw

Inner objective: Let this offer reopen the future I thought was gone.

- 0–12 seconds: Read the figure and go still for one beat. The next inhale is fuller. Lift the eyes toward the glass-room view, then return them to Potter.
- 12–28 seconds: At the trips, attention briefly goes elsewhere: he is imagining movement. Return quickly on “You're talking to me,” slightly embarrassed by how much Potter has seen. Allow actual delight, not just calculation.
- 28–42 seconds: “What happens to Bailey?” arrives fast, as if a good solution might make the whole arrangement possible. Potter's evasiveness interrupts that momentum, but it does not extinguish desire. The request to speak with Mary is sincere.
- 42–52 seconds: Take the hand while still anticipating the next step. Fingers tighten momentarily. The first “No” escapes; look directly at Potter as though needing to check who said it.
- 52–60 seconds: Hold the hand a fraction too long, release, and lose the clean sentence rhythm. The second refusals tumble over one another. Do not become poised or eloquent yet.

Watch for: pleasure must reach the body. Fail the interpretation if the offer remains merely intellectual or if he plays the handshake as supernatural horror.

Identity risks: elongating an already long face, beauty smoothing, changing eye shape, a different crown/hairline, making restraint read as inactivity. Preserve neck length and subtle jaw asymmetry.

## Test 2 — The models — target 50–55 seconds

Casting objective: Can anger become frightening, and can George discover its cost in his children's response without asking the audience to absolve him?

Scene state: George 40, Christmas Eve, immediately after the teacher call. Warm practical household light, natural shadows, no theatrical red tones. Eye-level medium-wide, 40mm lens impression, enough room to see George, the piano edge and model shelf together. Static single take. Children and Mary remain off-camera near lens height appropriate to their positions. Sparse, halting piano comes from its established location. Keep that geography identical in both readings.

Wardrobe: rumpled pale blue shirt, charcoal trousers, wedding band; dark winter coat within reach, petals already in its pocket. No bloodied lip yet; the bar assault happens later. Newel knob in his fist at the start; it is set on the piano and never thrown. Four chipboard models on the shelf: bridge, library, tower, train station. A single sweep breaks them. No laptop substituted. Keep model positions and debris trajectory consistent across takes.

Shared side and action, draft page 35:

[Janie begins playing softly after the call.]

GEORGE: Janie, stop it. Stop that!

[The piano stops. He draws back his arm with the knob, barely stops himself, and sets it on the piano. He turns to the models and sweeps them off the shelf. They break. He sees the family's faces and picks up his coat.]

GEORGE: I'm sorry. Janie — I'm sorry. Play it. Go on.

[He leaves. Hold briefly on the space he occupied.]

### Reading 2A — Daniel Reyes

Inner objective: Make the demands stop for one moment.

- 0–9 seconds: Piano resumes. Turn toward it too quickly. The first command is clipped; the second is much louder than the distance requires. His chest remains lifted as though still arguing with the caller.
- 9–20 seconds: Raise the knob. Catch the action late; keep the forearm rigid for a beat before lowering it. Place it on the piano with excessive care. This restraint does not resolve the anger.
- 20–29 seconds: Turn and clear the shelf in one fast sweep, with no explanatory glance toward Mary. The movement exceeds what his practical body usually permits itself.
- 29–42 seconds: Start to turn back as though another sentence is coming. See a child's off-camera face. Mouth opens; nothing comes. Look toward the other children and register that nobody is asking him for anything now.
- 42–55 seconds: Pick up the coat without meeting Mary's eyes. Apologize on insufficient breath. “Play it” is a failed attempt to restore the ordinary household rhythm. No piano resumes. Leave.

Watch for: the familiar capable man becomes someone whose next movement is uncertain. No self-pity before the family reaction, no charming rueful smile afterward.

### Reading 2B — Adrian Shaw

Inner objective: Stop the room from exposing the distance between my life and the life I imagined.

- 0–9 seconds: Try to hold still through the piano. Breath shortens, then the command breaks out abruptly. Keep it addressed to Janie, not delivered into empty space.
- 9–20 seconds: Lift the knob with an ugly, unfinished impulse. Stop, see what is in the hand, and set it down. Briefly press the lips together. Do not play successful self-control.
- 20–29 seconds: The eyes find the tower among the models for less than a second. Sweep the entire shelf with sudden force. No lingering farewell to the dream and no stylized close-up.
- 29–42 seconds: Watch a fragment settle. Lift the eyes expecting the familiar room; encounter the children's silence instead. The jaw loosens. He now understands that his private verdict on himself has become something they had to witness.
- 42–55 seconds: Pick up the coat. Attempt the first apology with habitual verbal precision; fail to sustain it. On Janie's name, the voice thins. “Go on” is almost a request. Leave without receiving reassurance.

Watch for: danger must remain immediate, not tastefully melancholic. He cannot explain the gesture with a long look at the models. Shame follows the damage.
