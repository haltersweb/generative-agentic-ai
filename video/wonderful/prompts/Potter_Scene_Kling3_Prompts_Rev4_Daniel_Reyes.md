Prepared 2026-09-29. Spoken words from the director's Potter sides v2, with "starting Monday" both times. Format from the Kling Working Guide v1. Only the text inside the grey code blocks goes into Kling.

## What changed from Rev 3

Rev 4 is about performance and pacing. The staging, camera and speaker rules are unchanged.

- **Subtext in every clip.** Each prompt now has an acting standard line (clearly visible but grounded, never exaggerated; calibrated on the approved C01) and an "Inner state" line, and the timeline turns that inner state into things the camera can see: where the eyes go, when the breath catches, when the face follows the thought.
- **Potter's lines are split into phrases** wherever George needs to react mid-line, using the timing that fixed C10. George reacts in the gaps, with his mouth closed, and doesn't compete with the speech.
- **An arc of comedy, then belief, then revulsion, then conviction.** The comedy is in the double take (C02), the fight to hold in a laugh (C05) and the mock search of the room (C06). Belief comes from recognition landing before the money (C08). The eyes change before the smile in C19. The final line is pity rather than rage.
- **Recurring business drawn from the performer concept.** He turns his wedding ring when he thinks of Mary (C04) and when he can't find his reason (C12), and one cheek creases when the smile is real.
- **The performer concept in the identity line.** Latino, 5'10", compact and broad-chested, warm medium-brown skin, close-set dark eyes, strong brows, slightly bent nose, broad mouth, substantial jaw, with barely any grey at 34. The concept's $310,000 figure is obsolete; the offer stays $300,000.
- **Tighter pacing.** Following the C03 and C09 lessons, tails are about 0.5 s and silent beats are 2.5 s or less, except where the action needs the room (C19, C25). Total raw runtime drops from about 166 s to 152 s.
